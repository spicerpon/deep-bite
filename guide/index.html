Deep Bite guide: how to grow fast and stay alive

Updated October 28, 2026 · by KOGAMES · Play Deep Bite · Update notes

Deep Bite is simple to start and surprisingly deep once you know the numbers behind it. This guide is written by the people who made the game, so every figure below comes straight from the balance file: what food is worth, how fast you move at each size, what boosting really costs, and how to turn all of that into a long run at the top of the leaderboard.

The one rule: 25%

Everything in Deep Bite comes back to one check. You can eat a fish only if your mass is at least 1.25× theirs, and the same test decides whether they can eat you. The number under each fish is its mass, so the maths is quick: a 100-mass fish can swallow anything at 80 or below, and is safe from anything at 124 or below.

That window between 80% and 125% of your own weight is the most interesting part of the map. Two fish in that band cannot hurt each other at all. They can bump, block and race for the same pellets, but nobody gets eaten. Learn to read the band and you stop panicking at every fish that looks a little bigger than you.

Drawn size lies. A fish's radius grows with the square root of its mass, so a fish that looks only slightly larger than you may be nearly double your weight. Always read the number, not the sprite.

What food is worth

About 2,000 pieces of food float around the ocean at any time, and each one respawns 9–13 seconds after it is eaten. Three kinds drift about:

Minnow+320%Tiny silver fish that wander. Best value per bite.
Pellets3–24 eachOnly after a deathWhen a fish dies, 30% of its mass scatters as golden pellets that last 45 seconds.

When you eat another fish you receive 60% of its mass immediately; another 30% turns into pellets around the spot, and the last 10% is gone. So a kill is worth far more than the hit itself: whoever cleans up the pellets afterwards often gains as much as the killer did.

Two things stand out. First, a newly spawned fish is almost 50% faster than a 10,000-mass monster, and turns twice as sharply. Small fish are never helpless; they are simply fast. Second, speed barely changes once you pass a few hundred mass, so from that point on the game is about positioning and reading the number, not about outrunning anyone.

Boost: when it pays off

Holding boost multiplies your speed by 1.75× and burns 3% of your mass every second (minimum 1). You cannot boost below 20 mass. Three uses make sense; everything else is a slow leak:

  • The strike. A fish you can eat is in front of you and about to turn away. One second of boost closes the gap; its mass more than pays for the 3% you spent.
  • The escape. Something 25% heavier is closing in. Boost at an angle, not straight away — big fish turn slowly, so a sharp turn plus a short burst breaks the chase almost every time.
  • The pellet race. A big fish just died nearby and the pellets are worth hundreds of mass. Being first matters more than the cost.

Boosting across empty water to "get somewhere faster" is the most common mistake we see. At 1,000 mass that trip costs 30 mass a second, which is ten minnows per second thrown away.

The first minute

  1. You spawn at 10 mass with 3 seconds of protection. Use it to turn towards the thickest cluster of plankton you can see, not towards other fish.
  2. Swim in gentle curves through food rather than chasing single pieces. At spawn size you are fast enough that a zig-zag through a cluster collects more than any straight line.
  3. Reach 40–50 mass before you bother with other fish. Below that, almost everything can eat you and nothing worthwhile is small enough for you to eat.
  4. Keep an eye on the minimap in the corner. Quiet areas near the map edge are the safest place to farm your first hundred.

Hunting without dying

Once you are past 100 mass, hunting is better value than grazing, but only if you choose targets well.

  • Hunt fish at 50–75% of your mass. They are worth the most of anything you are allowed to eat, and they are slower than the tiny ones, so you can actually catch them.
  • Cut corners, don't follow. Your target will curve. Aim for where it is going, and let its own turn bring it into your mouth.
  • Hunt away from bigger fish. The fish that can eat you will happily wait for you to commit to a chase, then come in while you are boosting in a straight line. Glance at the leaderboard: if the top three are near you on the minimap, go the other way.
  • Let the pellets come to you. After a kill, the scatter is yours only if nobody bigger is nearby. If a bigger fish arrives, leave immediately — pellets are not worth a death.

Holding first place

Above 300 mass your fish slowly slims down: every second you lose 0.12% of whatever you weigh above 300. At 1,000 mass that is less than one mass per second, but at 5,000 it is nearly six, and the leak never stops. Being huge is therefore a treadmill: you must keep eating just to stay still.

  • Patrol the middle of the map where food density and traffic are highest. Big fish see further (your view widens with size), so use that reach to spot kills early.
  • Stay off the walls. A big fish in a corner has nowhere to turn and becomes easy to box in.
  • Fish in your 80–125% band are your real rivals. You cannot eat each other, so the race is about who eats more of everything else. Herd them away from food rather than chasing them.
  • Do not boost out of boredom. At the top, boost is purely an escape tool; every burst is mass you then need to eat back.

Controls that matter

On desktop your fish follows the mouse, and you boost by holding the left button or Space. Keep the cursor close to your fish for tighter turns; a cursor far away just means "straight ahead". On phones a joystick appears under your thumb wherever you touch, and BOOST is the button on the right (or any second finger). Landscape gives you a wider view and is the better way to play on a phone.

Rooms, bots and continues

"Play Online" drops you into a shared ocean with up to 120 fish; when that fills up, a second ocean opens. If a room is quiet, computer-controlled fish keep the population around 22 so there is always something to hunt and something to fear; as real players join, the bots quietly leave. "Play vs Bots" runs the whole thing on your device with 34 bots and no connection at all.

When you are eaten you see your stats and can swim again at once. If you died with a decent amount of mass, you may also be offered an ad: watch it and you come back near where you died with half of your mass, once per life. It is worth it above a few hundred mass and rarely worth it below.

Quick answers

  • Why did a fish eat me when the numbers looked close? It needed to be only 25% heavier, and masses change every second as both of you eat. Keep a margin.
  • Do skins do anything? No. All ten skins are cosmetic; pick the one you can spot easily in a crowd.
  • Is there a maximum size? Mass caps at 30,000, but the decay above 300 means nobody has stayed near the cap for long.
  • Can I play with friends? Not by invitation yet — players who press Play Online at the same time usually land in the same ocean while it has room.
